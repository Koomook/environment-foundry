# Flow Protocol

The aim is not to look busy. It is to be where the body is: work while working,
be with family while with family, play while playing, and recover while
recovering.

## The four modes

Choose exactly one:

- `WORK` — one question, one artifact, one falsifier, and a visible finish.
- `PLAY` — enjoyment without a hidden obligation to appear productive.
- `RECOVER` — sleep, food, walking, stillness, or light movement; not overflow.
- `DECIDE` — a bounded decision package ending in `COMMIT`, `EXPERIMENT`,
  `PARK`, or `KILL`.

## Ninety-second reset

1. Name the current mode and the mode your body is actually in.
2. If they differ, stop adding shame. Close the conflicting surface.
3. Choose one honest block:
   - ten minutes to restart a small work artifact;
   - a declared play block;
   - a recovery block;
   - or a decision package.
4. Write the finish condition before starting.
5. When the block ends, stop or deliberately choose the next block.

“Sitting at work while consuming shorts, webtoons, or feeds” is not counted as
work or rest. It is a transition failure. Repair the environment first:
stand up, drink water, put the distracting surface out of reach, and return
with a ten-minute artifact or declare rest.

## Long-horizon test

Before repeating a pattern, compare the likely result at seven, thirty, and
ninety days across separate dimensions:

- meaningful work;
- family presence;
- sleep and physical capacity;
- learning;
- trust in oneself;
- reversibility.

Do not silently trade one dimension for another. If the forecast depends on
chronic sleep loss, broken family anchors, concealed work, or self-disgust as
fuel, narrow the plan.

## Weekly learning

Review outcomes, not claimed effort:

- Which blocks produced a real artifact?
- Which play or family blocks were fully inhabited?
- What repeatedly caused transition failure?
- What environmental change worked?
- Which forecast was wrong?

Change the environment before changing the Constitution. Change a
constitutional article only with evidence, counterexample review, and the
required assent.
